4 The six menu structures

The independent variable is menu structure, not a cosmetic theme. All six structures expose the same information architecture and the same products, but they differ in geometry, visible breadth, click depth, spatial persistence, and motor demands. This section defines each structure precisely enough to build it, then specifies the controls that keep the comparison fair.

4.1 Shared information architecture

The store is a fictional watch retailer with four departments, three subcategories per department, and six leaf styles per subcategory — 72 products in total. Every menu renders this same tree; only the presentation changes. The running example throughout is Sport & Dive → Pro Diver 300m → Titanium Deep.

4.2 Operational definitions and wireframes

The wireframes below are interaction specifications, not final visual designs. They show what is visible at each step, where the click targets are, and how the user moves between levels. The build must preserve these structural properties; the visual skin can be refined in the pilot as long as parity controls (§4.3) are respected.

4.3 Parity and confound control

The structures are intentionally different, so some confounds are intrinsic. The goal is not to make them identical — that would destroy the manipulation — but to ensure that every difference is either part of the construct or measured and modelled. Table 4.2 is the build contract.

Table 4.2 — What is held constant, what varies, and how it is handled
PropertyControlWhy / analysis consequence

PersistenceB keeps the tree visible; A/C are transient; D/F replace the viewport; E replaces the grid.Persistence is part of the construct (wayfinding). Measure perceived persistence and use it in mediation.

The click-depth problem — state it, do not hide it

The mega menu (C) reaches a leaf in two clicks; the other five need three. That is a real advantage of mega menus and a plausible reason frustrated users like them. But it means a C-vs-A difference cannot be attributed to “layout” alone. The analysis therefore reports (a) the raw structural comparison, (b) a mediation analysis with clicks as the mediator, and (c) a matched-depth sensitivity analysis on A/B/D/E/F only. If the C advantage disappears when clicks are equated, the paper says so. This is more honest than adding a fake third click to C and pretending the menus are equal.

Academic-integrity note

Two of the six menu attributions in the brief could not be verified. The plan keeps the designs because they are well-defined and testable, but it does not claim that Chiossi et al. or Collins et al. reported the effects described. Before submitting the paper, either obtain the PDFs and verify the claims or remove the attributions. The reference list marks both as “unverified — do not cite as evidence.”
