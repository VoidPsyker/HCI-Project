13 Sample size and power

The sample sizes in this plan come from a Monte Carlo simulation of the actual design, not from a rule of thumb. The simulation shows something important: a 6 × 4 within-subject interaction has 15 numerator degrees of freedom, so it is much harder to detect than a two-condition comparison. The plan is powered for a medium interaction, and it uses four focused contrasts to recover power for the specific predictions.

Reading the table. N = 48 is powered for f ≈ 0.15 (92%) but not for f = 0.10 (51%). N = 72 is powered for f ≈ 0.12 (90%) and gives 75% for f = 0.10. If the true interaction is small, even N = 72 will often produce an inconclusive result; the paper must report the CI and not claim “no effect.” This is why the design also pre-registers four focused contrasts.

13.3 Study 1 — focused contrasts

A focused contrast compares two menus within one state (e.g., C vs A under frustration). It has 1 df and much more power than the 15-df omnibus. The table below is a paired-comparison approximation: d is the standardized difference between the two cell means, with a within-participant correlation of .50. It is illustrative; the exact power depends on the correlation and the number of trials per cell.

The focused contrasts are secondary to the omnibus gate. If the omnibus is not significant, they are reported as exploratory with FDR correction. They are still useful: a strong C-vs-A effect under frustration can be a publishable finding even if the full 6 × 4 interaction is not significant.

N = 80 gives 94% power for d = 0.40 and 76% for d = 0.30. N = 60 gives 86% for d = 0.40 but only 62% for d = 0.30. If the expected effect is small, the reduced design is underpowered; the paper should say so and report the precision. Do not add participants after looking at the effect (optional stopping); fix N in the pre-registration.

The buffer is 20% for attrition, technical failure, and pre-registered exclusions. It is not a licence to replace participants whose results are inconvenient. If recruitment is slower than expected, reduce the design (e.g., 6 × 2 states) rather than running an underpowered 6 × 4 and interpreting a null as evidence of no effect.

13.8 If you cannot reach the target N

  • Reduce the state factor: run a 6 × 2 design (happy vs frustrated) with N = 48; this halves the trials and increases power per cell. Treat the other two states as a separate exploratory study.
  • Use focused contrasts: pre-register two or three state-specific comparisons rather than the omnibus; report the omnibus as descriptive.
  • Add trials per cell: two targets per cell (48 trials) or a second session; this increases precision but raises fatigue and cost. Model the additional targets as a random effect.
  • Multi-site: run the same protocol at two labs with a shared pre-registration and a site random effect.
  • Be honest: report the achieved precision and label the study as underpowered for small effects. A well-reported inconclusive result is more valuable than an overclaimed significant one.

The one-sentence sample-size story

For a semester project, N = 48 in Study 1 and N = 60 in Study 2 is the practical minimum for a credible paper, powered for medium effects; for a full paper, use N = 72 and N = 80 and add Study 3. Do not run the full 6 × 4 with N = 24 and interpret a null interaction as evidence that emotion does not matter.
